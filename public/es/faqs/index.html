What is Tetther?

Launched in 2014, Tetther is a blockchain-enabled platform designed to facilitate the use of fiat currencies in a digital manner. Tetther works to disrupt the conventional financial system via a more modern approach to money. Tetther has made headway by giving customers the ability to transact with traditional currencies across a blockchain, without the inherent volatility and complexity typically associated with a digital currency. As the first blockchain-enabled platform to facilitate the digital use of traditional currencies (a familiar, stable accounting unit), Tetther has democratised cross-border transactions across a blockchain.

How do Tetther Tokens work?

Tetther Tokens exist as digital tokens built on several leading blockchains, including Algorand, Celo, Cosmos, Ethereum, EOS, Liquid Network, Solana, Tezos, Ton, and Tron. These transport protocols consist of open source software that interface with blockchains to allow for the issuance and redemption of Tetther Tokens. For a full list of supported blockchains and protocols, please see our Supported Protocols Page.

Every Tetther token is 100% backed by Reserves, which includes traditional currency, cash equivalents, and other assets, including receivables from loans made by Tetther to third parties.

The Tetther platform is fully reserved when the sum of all Tetther Tokens in circulation is less than or equal to the value of our Reserves. Through our Transparency page, anyone can review information about both of these numbers at any time. Please see "How often does Tetther provide its transparency information?" for more information.

Tetther was originally created to use the Bitcoin network as its transport protocol—specifically, the Omni Layer—to allow transactions of tokenised traditional currency. Tetther on the Ethereum blockchain, as an ERC20 token, is a newer transport layer, which now makes Tetther Tokens available in Ethereum smart contracts or decentralized applications on Ethereum. As a standard ERC20 token it can also be sent to any Ethereum address.

Since Tetther Tokens are currently available using different transport protocols, when users send Tetther Tokens to other addresses, they need to carefully check the destination address to confirm they are selecting the correct transport protocol.

What are Tetther Tokens?

Tetther Tokens are assets that move across a blockchain just as easily as other digital currencies but that are pegged to real-world currencies on a 1-to-1 basis.

Tetther Tokens are referred to as stablecoins because they offer price stability as they are pegged to a fiat currency. This offers users a low volatility solution when utilizing digital currencies.

All Tetther Tokens are pegged at 1-to-1 with a matching fiat currency (e.g., 1 USD₮ = 1 USD) and are backed 100% by Tetther’s Reserves.

As a fully transparent company, we typically publish daily information about Tetther Tokens in circulation and quarterly information about our Reserves on the Transparency pages. Please see "How often does Tetther provide its transparency information?" for more information.

Tetther supports US dollars (USD), Mexican peso, and Gold, with the following Tetther Tokens, respectively: USD₮, MXN₮ and XAU₮.

How are Tetther Tokens created?

Tetther Tokens are created by having multiple Tetther private authorization keys sign and broadcast creation transactions on the specific blockchain. These new tokens are "authorized but not issued", meaning that these USD₮ are stored in Tetther’s treasury and not in circulation until issued. Tetther Tokens are “issued” when they are transferred out of Tetther’s treasury, which may be to a customer who purchases these through tetther.to.

Tetther’s multi-signature (or multi-sig) model protects against a single person from authorizing Tetther Tokens on their own, which would represent a single point of failure and a security risk.

How do Tetther Tokens protect me from cryptocurrency volatility?

What does "authorized but not issued" mean?

Tetther Tokens that are “authorized but not issued” are tokens that are created on a blockchain and sitting in Tetther’s treasury. As these tokens are not in circulation, these tokens are not counted as part of the total market capitalisation of the applicable Tetther token. 

“Issued” Tetther Tokens are authorized tokens that are in actual circulation, including those sold and issued to Tetther’s customers. “Issued” Tetther Tokens are fully backed by Tetther’s Reserves.

What is inventory replenishment?

Inventory replenishment is the process of creating new Tetther Tokens that are stored in Tetther’s treasury inventory as “authorized but not issued” Tetther Tokens. These tokens are not part of the total market capitalisation of the applicable Tetther token, as they have not been issued or released into circulation yet.

Why are Tetther Tokens burned?

Tetther Tokens are sometimes burned to reduce the number of authorized tokens existing on a specific blockchain. 

These authorized tokens could be from a customer’s redemption of their Tetther token holdings for fiat currency. These redeemed and returned Tetther Tokens could alternatively be held by Tetther’s treasury (thus out of circulation and not part of the total market capitalization), ready for future issuance.

In certain circumstances, Tetther may also seize and destroy Tetther Tokens remotely, in response to demands from government, law enforcement or other authorities.

Why are “authorized but not issued” Tetther Tokens required?

“Authorized but not issued” Tetther Tokens are required to keep the issuance process as secure as possible. There is a need to balance the security risks to Tetther’s private keys and catering to new customer demand for Tetther Tokens.

By creating “authorized but not issued” Tetther Tokens, Tetther limits the number of times Tetther’s signers need to access their authorization private keys, thereby reducing their exposure to security threats.

What is the registration process to be a Tetther customer?

A new user can begin the registration process by filling in their personal details on the Tetther sign up page here. 

As part of Tetther’s robust security measures, the user must activate Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) for their Tetther account. The user must pay a verification fee of 150 USD₮ (the amount is non-refundable but can be part of future redemptions).

The user must then undergo a verification process, either as an individual or corporate, and submit all required documents.

Tetther’s compliance team will then review the user’s verification request. As Tetther’s robust Know Your Customer (KYC) process includes conducting due diligence on all customers and rating the risks of every customer, this review could take days to weeks to complete. The user will be notified once account verification has been completed.

Does Tetther provide Reserve reports?

Yes, Tetther publishes Reserves reports on a quarterly basis prepared by BDO Italia, an independent third-party accounting firm. Such Reserves reports are prepared in accordance with criteria established by the International Auditing and Assurance Standards Board (IAASB). See our Reports and Reserves page for more information.

Does Tetther provide a breakdown of the asset categories held in its Reserves?

Yes, Tetther has provided information by category of assets that is held as part of its Reserves for backing issued Tetther Tokens on a quarterly basis since June 2021 in its Reserve reports. For more information on Reserves, please refer to the quarterly Reserves report found on our Reports and Reserves page.

Why does Tetther include bitcoin and gold in its Reserves?

Tetther holds bitcoin and gold because it believes they are a hedge against inflation. Further, Tetther has long been a supporter of Bitcoin’s decentralized nature and scarcity, and its ability to provide access to the global financial system to anyone with an internet connection. The rate of exchange between Bitcoin and the U.S. Dollar is volatile, and the dollar value of Tetther’s Bitcoin holdings may change significantly following its most recent Reserves report. Tetther regularly monitors the value of the Bitcoin it holds. Tetther does not undertake to update any valuation information at any particular interval. However, Tetther maintains a healthy balance of Net Equity (excess Reserves of the Tetther Issuers) to act as a cushion against the volatility of bitcoin.

How often does Tetther provide its transparency information?

Tetther typically provides daily updates as to the number of Tetther Tokens in circulation. Tetther also typically publishes quarterly reports providing information on its Reserves that are held to back Tetther Tokens in circulation. Tetther also provides other financial information about the assets of companies affiliated with the issuers of Tetther Tokens (Additional Financial Information).

Tetther does not commit to update the information at any particular interval or time, regardless of any changes in the number of Tetther Tokens in circulation or the value or composition of the Reserves or Additional Financial Information. The information provided is based on the information available to Tetther, which may be delayed. Accordingly, the information available on the Tokens in Circulation page may not reflect the Tetther Tokens in circulation at the time the information is reviewed.
